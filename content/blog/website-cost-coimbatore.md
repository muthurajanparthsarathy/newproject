"How much for a website?" is the first question almost every business owner asks us — and the honest answer is *it depends on what the website needs to do*. This guide explains what changes the price, what you'll pay every year after launch, and what to ask before you hire anyone.

## The short answer

For a small business in Coimbatore, most websites fall into three groups:

| Type of website | Good for | Our starting price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Static website** | Shops, salons, clinics, service businesses that need to be found and contacted | from ₹7,000 |
| **Dynamic website** | Businesses that update content often — blogs, menus, listings, bookings | from ₹10,000 |
| **Online store** | Selling products with online payments and order tracking | from ₹15,000 |

These are one-time build prices. Your domain, hosting and any paid services (like payment gateways or business email) are billed separately at actual cost.

## What makes a website cost more

### 1. Number of pages

A five-page site (Home, About, Services, Gallery, Contact) is very different from a 30-page site with a page for every product or location. More pages means more design, more writing and more testing.

### 2. Static or dynamic

A **static** site shows the same content to everyone and changes only when a developer updates it. It's fast, secure and cheap to run.

A **dynamic** site has a database and an admin area, so *you* can add blog posts, products or offers yourself. That flexibility costs more to build — but saves you from paying for every small change later.

### 3. Custom design vs a ready-made look

A layout built around your brand, photos and customers takes more time than adapting an existing design. If your website is your main source of leads, custom design is usually worth it.

### 4. Features

Each of these adds work:

- Online payments (Razorpay, UPI, cards)
- Appointment or table booking
- Customer logins
- Connections to billing, inventory or CRM software
- Multiple languages (for example English and Tamil)

### 5. Content: photos and words

The most common reason websites get delayed is missing content. If you already have good photos and text, the project moves fast. If you need a product shoot or copywriting, budget for it separately — stock photos rarely sell a local business well.

## Costs after launch

A website isn't a one-time expense. Plan for:

- **Domain name** — a small yearly renewal.
- **Hosting** — static sites are cheap to host; dynamic sites and online stores need more capable (paid) hosting.
- **Maintenance** — security updates, backups and small text or photo changes. You can do some of this yourself on a dynamic site, or keep a monthly maintenance plan.

Ask any agency to write these running costs down *before* you start, so there are no surprises in year two.

## Questions to ask before you hire anyone

1. **Will I own the domain and the website?** The domain should be registered in your name.
2. **Can I edit content myself?** If yes, ask for a demo of the admin area.
3. **Is it mobile-friendly?** Most of your customers will visit on their phone.
4. **Is basic SEO included?** Page titles, descriptions, a sitemap and Google Search Console setup should be standard.
5. **How many rounds of changes are included?**
6. **What happens after launch?** Who fixes things if something breaks?
